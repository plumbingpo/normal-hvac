From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, everything happens in a strict order: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower moving heat through the ducts. Diagnosis is mostly a matter of finding where that chain breaks. In Normal homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Our experienced technicians diagnose a furnace the way it operates, in order, with a meter at every step. That means igniter resistance, microamp flame signal, pressure switch operation under actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor condition. Safety checks are never optional on a gas appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak testing on the gas connections, and flue draft verification, since a hazard rarely shows itself at the thermostat. We finish by checking temperature rise against the data plate, making sure the burner and blower work together the way the manufacturer designed them to.

Conventional or condensing, gas, propane, or electric, we repair them all, across every major brand, including the traps, drains, and dual pressure switches that come with high-efficiency models. The parts most likely to fail are stocked on every truck: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors. Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Air moving through the vents that never gets warm
  • A bang or boom when the burners ignite late
  • Clicking and a glowing igniter with no burner flame
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower
  • Burners light, run a few seconds, then shut down repeatedly
  • Soot, rust, or moisture near the furnace cabinet or vent pipe
  • A pilot or burner flame that is lazy yellow instead of crisp blue
  • Short cycling every few minutes without reaching the setpoint

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

The failure that matters most in a furnace is usually hidden. A cracked or corroded heat exchanger can let combustion gases into the airstream, and nothing about it is visible from the outside of the cabinet. Delayed ignition makes things worse, because each boom at light-off puts more stress on that metal. Add high voltage, a gas valve, and a flue to the same box, and there is little room for guesswork. Diagnosis is full of traps too. A high limit that keeps tripping could be a bad switch, or it could be doing exactly its job because a filter is clogged or the blower is dying, and swapping out a working switch only hides the real fault.

At Normal HVAC, no symptom gets treated in isolation. A meter tells us the flame signal, so we never condemn a good sensor while the real culprit, a cracked igniter or a weak gas valve, stays in place. We confirm draft at both the pressure switch and the flue, measure temperature rise against the data plate to flag airflow problems early, and test the gas side with a leak detector, not a guess. When we are done, the furnace has proven itself safe and correct through a full cycle, and if it is nearing the end of its useful life, you will get an honest assessment.

How to Prevent Furnace Repair Problems

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • If you have a high-efficiency furnace, check that the condensate drain is flowing freely
  • Book a fall tune-up so weak igniters and fouled sensors are caught before the first freeze
  • Keep storage, lint, and especially chlorine-based products away from the furnace closet
  • Replace the filter every month while the furnace is working hard, since it costs less than any other part
  • Pay attention when the burners light, since a boom instead of a soft whoosh calls for service
  • Avoid packing the utility room, because burners need open space for combustion air
  • Test your CO detectors monthly and make sure each floor has one
